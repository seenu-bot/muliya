The Complete Buyer Checklist for Residential Plots in Kanakapura Road
🎯  KEYWORD: Residential Plot in Kanakapura Road Bangalore for Sale, Residential plots for sale in Kanakapura Road
Use this checklist in order. Every point applies to any residential plot in Kanakapura Road Bangalore for sale — whether it is in a gated community, an open layout, or a resale transaction.

Step 1 — Shortlist Based on Location and Budget
Before contacting any developer or seller, fix your non-negotiables: micro-location preference (Kaggalipura vs NICE Road belt vs peripheral), minimum plot size, approval type (BMRDA only, or open to BDA), and total available budget including registration and construction. Buyers who shortlist without this clarity often waste time evaluating projects that do not match their actual requirements.

Step 2 — Verify BMRDA/BDA Layout Approval
This is the single most important step. For any residential plots for sale in Kanakapura Road, request the BMRDA layout sanction number from the developer and verify it directly on the official BMRDA portal. The approval order should mention the layout name, survey numbers, total area, and sanctioned plot count. Cross-check that the survey numbers in the approval order match the plot being sold.

Step 3 — Check DC Conversion
Agricultural land must have a valid DC (Deputy Commissioner) conversion order before it can be used for residential development. Ask to see the conversion certificate and verify the date, survey number, and nature of conversion. Without this, the layout is technically on agricultural land — which creates legal complications for construction and resale.

Step 4 — Verify Title Deed and Ownership History
Request a certified copy of the title deed and trace the ownership history for at least 20–30 years. A clean chain of ownership with no gaps is what you are looking for. Any unexplained gap, court order reference, or disputed period in the ownership history requires independent legal verification before proceeding.

Step 5 — Obtain and Review the Encumbrance Certificate
An Encumbrance Certificate (EC) for 15–30 years confirms that the land has no loans, mortgages, court attachments, or disputes registered against it. Obtain the EC from the Sub-Registrar's office independently — do not rely on the developer to provide it. Cross-check the survey number on the EC with the plot being sold.

Step 6 — Confirm RERA Registration
Any developer of a plotted community with more than the specified number of plots must register the project under RERA Karnataka. Verify the RERA number on the official RERA Karnataka portal. RERA registration protects buyers against delivery delays, specification changes, and developer default.

Step 7 — Confirm e-Khata Eligibility
Ask the developer explicitly: will individual e-Khata be issued in the buyer's name at the time of registration? e-Khata is the Karnataka government's digital land record system — it is what proves legal ownership after purchase. Developers who are confident in their legal standing will commit to this clearly.

Step 8 — Visit the Site — More Than Once
A site visit is not optional for a purchase of this size. Visit at least twice — once in normal weather and once during or after rainfall to check drainage and waterlogging. First visits are often curated — second visits reveal the reality.

Step 9 — Verify the Developer's Track Record
Ask for a list of at least 2–3 previously completed projects and visit them. Speak to existing residents if possible. Ask how closely the delivered project matched the brochure. A developer with a strong delivery track record for premium residential plots in Kanakapura Road is a meaningful differentiator at a time when multiple new developers are entering this corridor.

Step 10 — Independent Legal Verification
Before signing any agreement or paying any token amount, commission an independent property lawyer to verify all documents — title deed, EC, BMRDA approval, conversion certificate, and RERA registration. This typically costs ₹15,000–₹25,000 and can save lakhs in future disputes. Developer-provided legal opinion is not a substitute for independent verification.
